# Smartiflix Final Editorial Originality Audit

Audit date: 2026-10-12

## Method and limits

The review covered all 459 reader-visible section paragraphs across the ten article records. It included exact normalized-paragraph matching, repeated-heading detection, the existing 8-word shingle near-duplicate script, manual cluster review, search-intent comparison, and targeted exact-phrase web searches for distinctive passages. No external search can prove that text is plagiarism-free across the entire internet; search results and source comparisons were used only to identify obvious attribution or wording risks.

The exact paragraph scan found zero duplicate paragraph groups. The project near-duplicate checker found zero substantial article pairs. One repeated heading was found inside the Smart TV player article and was renamed from `Google TV and Android TV` to `Google TV and Android TV Player Criteria`. The generic heading `Troubleshooting` occurs in two different platform articles but is supported by different device-specific copy and was retained.

## External originality checks

Targeted searches of distinctive phrases were used for representative Firestick, EPG, buffering, and Apple TV passages. No evidence was found that the reviewed Smartiflix paragraphs reproduce a source passage verbatim. This is a limited web spot-check, not a plagiarism certification. Platform documentation is linked where factual claims depend on it, and the article prose is not presented as copied documentation.

## Remaining editorial concerns

- Several articles necessarily share a vocabulary—player, playlist, EPG, credentials, network, and support—because the cluster covers connected troubleshooting tasks. The articles differ in intent, sequence, and decision criteria.
- FAQ answers intentionally use a small amount of shared safety language. FAQs are excluded from the strict editorial count and are not treated as evidence of article originality.
- The article data contains many H2 sections because the original brief required deep coverage. Future editorial maintenance should consolidate sections only if readability data shows the long structure is harming readers.
- No personal testing, rankings, benchmarks, user reviews, or expert credentials are claimed.
